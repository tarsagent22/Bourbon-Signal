## Expansion economics and what reuse should prove

The five v2 pairs cover Remedy Glendale, Main Street El Cajon, Christie's La Crescenta, Vino Lexington and Hot Spot Austin. The release worktree's reviewed v2 manifests sum to **10 GETs and 30,111 response-body bytes per five-seed round**: Remedy 5,018; Vino 4,996; Main Street 11,400; Hot Spot 3,868; Christie's 4,829. A 30-minute model is 480 GETs and 1,445,328 body bytes/day, excluding headers, operational verification, discovery, failures and future growth. These are fixture-derived estimates, not production measurements or permission to change cadence. Del Mesa's recovered catalog run is broader and substantially larger; its seven rows are not seven stores or a directly comparable rare-bottle depth guarantee.

Report onboarding work as reviewed data changes: exact HTTPS origin/section family; source/merchant IDs; full premises including unit, city/state/ZIP; product/variant/handle/title/size; real canonical and current state classification; narrow title/heading exceptions; both response URLs/status/time/bytes/hashes; source clock, budgets and complete-negative semantics; immutable admission and baseline references. Record exceptions and elapsed onboarding effort. The unchanged collector must onboard at least three of stores two through five; synthetic approval hashes do not satisfy real admission.

For the next wave, favor a multi-premise response: the existing collector has capacity for at most five reviewed premises per variant using the same product/variant pair, if **every configured premise is actually named exactly once**. That could amortize two requests across several locations. The current five positive field fixtures each contain one premise; obtain and review a genuine positive multi-premise fixture before claiming that efficiency in operation. Missing branches must fail the complete-premises check, never inherit another store's positive availability.
